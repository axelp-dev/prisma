# PRISMA Mathematical Formulation

This document provides the mathematical derivations, physical hypotheses, and numerical schemes governing microscale particle transport inside a biaxial Random Positioning Machine (RPM).

---

## 1. Reference Frames & Kinematics

Modeling cellular or colloidal suspensions in a Random Positioning Machine requires establishing the kinematic relationships across multiple rotating reference frames. The primary objective of the RPM is to continuously randomize the apparent orientation of the static gravitational vector relative to the sample frame, driving its time-averaged value toward zero.

### 1.1 Frame Definitions

- **$\mathcal{R}_0 = (O; \vec{x}_0, \vec{y}_0, \vec{z}_0)$ (Laboratory Frame)**: Inertial reference frame. The static terrestrial gravity vector is:
  $$\vec{g}_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ -g \end{pmatrix}_{\mathcal{R}_0} \quad \text{with } g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$$
- **$\mathcal{R}_1$ (Outer Frame / Tilt)**: Rotates by angle $\theta_2$ around the horizontal axis $\vec{y}_0$.
- **$\mathcal{R}_2 = (O; \vec{x}_2, \vec{y}_2, \vec{z}_2)$ (Inner Frame / Sample Cuvette)**: Rotates by angle $\theta_1$ around the gimbal axis $\vec{z}_1$ (coinciding with $\vec{z}_0$ at rest $\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0$).

### 1.2 Coordinate Rotation Matrix

The complete orientation transformation from $\mathcal{R}_0$ (lab) to $\mathcal{R}_2$ (sample) is governed by the canonical sequence:
$$R = R_z(\theta_1) R_y(-\theta_2)$$

> **Convention note:** In standard right-handed kinematics consistent with `CanonicalTwoAxis`, the tilt rotation around $\vec{y}_0$ is parameterized as $R_y(-\theta_2)$ so that at rest ($\theta_1 = \theta_2 = 0$), the gravity vector points strictly along $-\vec{z}_2$.

Evaluating the matrix product:
$$\begin{align*} R &=  \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta_1 & -\sin\theta_1 & 0 \\ \sin\theta_1 & \cos\theta_1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta_2 & 0 & -\sin\theta_2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ \sin\theta_2 & 0 & \cos\theta_2 \end{pmatrix} \\ &=  \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta_1 \cos\theta_2 & -\sin\theta_1 & -\cos\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \\ \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_2 & \cos\theta_1 & -\sin\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \\ \sin\theta_2 & 0 & \cos\theta_2 \end{pmatrix} \end{align*}$$

The apparent gravity vector $\vec{g}_{\mathcal{R}_2}(t)$ experienced in the moving sample frame is:
$$\vec{g}_{\mathcal{R}_2}(t) = R(t) \vec{g}_0 = g \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \\ \sin\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \\ -\cos\theta_2 \end{pmatrix}$$

### 1.3 Instantaneous Angular Velocity & Acceleration

Expressed in the moving frame $\mathcal{R}_2$, the angular velocity vector $\vec{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}_2}$ reads:
$$\vec{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}_2} = \begin{pmatrix} -\dot{\theta}_2 \sin\theta_1 \\ \dot{\theta}_2 \cos\theta_1 \\ \dot{\theta}_1 \end{pmatrix}_{\mathcal{R}_2}$$

The instantaneous angular acceleration is computed via numerical differentiation:
$$\dot{\vec{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{R}_2} = \frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{\Omega}_{\mathcal{R}_2}}{\mathrm{d}t}$$

> **Physical note:** The derivative $\dot{\vec{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{R}_2}$ governs the fictitious Euler force, acting perpendicularly to position whenever motor velocities vary.

## 2. Dynamic Force Balance in Non-Inertial Frame

Consider a spherical particle of radius $R$ and density $\rho_p$ suspended in a fluid of density $\rho_f$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu$. 

The inclusion experiences four competing force systems:
1. Linear viscous Stokes drag.
2. Buoyancy generated by the apparent gravity field.
3. Non-inertial fictitious forces (centrifugal, Euler, Coriolis).
4. Hydrodynamic virtual mass (added-mass effect: $m_{\text{add}} = \frac{1}{2}\rho_f V_p$).

The generalized momentum balance in $\mathcal{R}_2$ is:
$$m^* \frac{\mathrm{d}^2\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = -6\pi\mu R \frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t} + (m_p - m_f)\left( \vec{g}_{\mathcal{R}_2}(t) - \vec{\Omega} \times (\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{r}) - \dot{\vec{\Omega}} \times \vec{r} \right) - 2 m_p \left( \vec{\Omega} \times \frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t} \right) + \vec{F}_{\text{ext}}$$

where:
- $V_p = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$ is the inclusion volume.
- $m_p = \rho_p V_p$ is the particle mass.
- $m_f = \rho_f V_p$ is the displaced fluid mass.
- $m^* = m_p + \frac{1}{2}m_f = V_p (\rho_p + \frac{1}{2}\rho_f)$ is the virtual mass.

### Dimensionless Coefficients

Dividing by the virtual inertia factor $(\rho_p + \frac{1}{2}\rho_f)$ yields:
- **Viscous relaxation timescale:**
  $$\tau_v = \frac{2}{9} \frac{R^2 (\rho_p + \frac{1}{2}\rho_f)}{\mu}$$
- **Buoyancy / density contrast factor:**
  $$\beta = \frac{\rho_p - \rho_f}{\rho_p + \frac{1}{2}\rho_f}$$
- **Coriolis factor:**
  $$\gamma = \frac{\rho_p}{\rho_p + \frac{1}{2}\rho_f}$$

## 3. The Overdamped Stokes Regime ($Re_p \ll 1$)

### 3.1 Particle Reynolds Number

The particle Reynolds number represents the ratio of fluid convective inertial forces to viscous shear forces:
$$Re_p = \frac{\rho_f \Vert{}\vec{v}\Vert{} (2R)}{\mu}$$

When $Re_p \ll 0.1$ (empirically $Re_p \sim 10^{-5}$ in viscous media), linear laminar Stokes drag dominates, and boundary layer separation is nonexistent.

### 3.2 Timescale Separation & Asymptotic Reduction

The viscous relaxation timescale satisfies:
$$\tau_v \sim 10^{-6}\text{ s to } 3\times 10^{-3}\text{ s} \ll T_{\text{RPM}} \sim 1\text{ to } 10\text{ s}$$

Because $\tau_v$ is orders of magnitude smaller than the characteristic rotation period of the machine:
1. Relative particle momentum decays almost instantaneously.
2. The acceleration term $\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$ and Coriolis forces are negligible.
3. The particle continuously resides at its dynamic terminal equilibrium velocity.

Setting $\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t^2} \approx \vec{0}$ reduces the second-order stiff ODE to an un-stiff first-order equation:
$$\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \vec{v}_{\text{limit}}(\vec{r}, t)$$

where:
$$\vec{v}_{\text{limit}}(\vec{r}, t) = \tau_v \beta \left( \vec{g}_{\mathcal{R}_2}(t) - \vec{\Omega} \times (\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{r}) - \dot{\vec{\Omega}} \times \vec{r} \right)$$

## 4. Elastic Restoring Force (Cytoskeleton Coupling)

For organelles linked to an actin filament network (e.g., statoliths in plant statocytes), an isotropic restoring force toward anchor point $\vec{r}_0$ is introduced:
$$\vec{F}_{\text{elast}} = -k (\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0)$$

Introducing the elastic relaxation time $\tau_k = \frac{6\pi\mu R}{k}$:
$$\vec{v}_{\text{limit}}(\vec{r}, t) = \tau_v \beta \left( \vec{g}_{\mathcal{R}_2}(t) - \vec{\Omega} \times (\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{r}) - \dot{\vec{\Omega}} \times \vec{r} \right) - \frac{\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0}{\tau_k}$$

## 5. Boundary Conditions (Spherical Cellular Confinement)

Let a spherical cell boundary have radius $R_{\text{cell}}$ centered at $\vec{c}$. The valid spatial domain for a spherical particle center of radius $R_p$ is:
$$\Vert{}\vec{r} - \vec{c}\Vert{} \le R_{\text{max}} = R_{\text{cell}} - R_p$$

### Inelastic Sliding Boundary Rule
In low Reynolds creeping flows, restitution coefficients vanish (purely inelastic collision without rebound).

Let $\vec{n} = \frac{\vec{r} - \vec{c}}{\Vert{}\vec{r} - \vec{c}\Vert{}}$ be the outward unit normal vector.
If $\Vert{}\vec{r} - \vec{c}\Vert{} \ge R_{\text{max}}$ and $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n} > 0$:
1. Geometric repositioning: $\vec{r} \leftarrow \vec{c} + R_{\text{max}} \vec{n}$
2. Normal velocity cancellation: $\vec{v} \leftarrow \vec{v} - (\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n})\vec{n}$

## 4. Numerical Integration Scheme (RK4)

The state equation $\frac{\mathrm{d}\vec{r}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \vec{v}_{\text{limit}}(\vec{r}, t)$ is integrated over $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ with step $\Delta t = t_{n+1} - t_n$ using an explicit 4th-order Runge-Kutta scheme:

$$\begin{aligned} \vec{k}_1 &= \vec{v}_{\text{limit}}(\vec{r}_n, t_n) \\ \vec{k}_2 &= \vec{v}_{\text{limit}}\left(\vec{r}_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\vec{k}_1, t_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\right) \\ \vec{k}_3 &= \vec{v}_{\text{limit}}\left(\vec{r}_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\vec{k}_2, t_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}\right) \\ \vec{k}_4 &= \vec{v}_{\text{limit}}(\vec{r}_n + \Delta t \, \vec{k}_3, t_{n+1}) \\ \vec{r}_{n+1} &= \vec{r}_n + \frac{\Delta t}{6}(\vec{k}_1 + 2\vec{k}_2 + 2\vec{k}_3 + \vec{k}_4) \end{aligned}$$

Mid-step kinematic quantities ($\vec{g}_{\mathcal{R}_2}, \vec{\Omega}, \dot{\vec{\Omega}}$) at $t_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}$ are linearly interpolated:
$$\begin{aligned} \vec{g}_{\text{mid}} &= \frac{1}{2}(\vec{g}_n + \vec{g}_{n+1}) \\ \vec{\Omega}_{\text{mid}} &= \frac{1}{2}(\vec{\Omega}_n + \vec{\Omega}_{n+1}) \\ \dot{\vec{\Omega}}_{\text{mid}} &= \frac{1}{2}(\dot{\vec{\Omega}}_n + \dot{\vec{\Omega}}_{n+1}) \end{aligned}$$
